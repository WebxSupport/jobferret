# 03 — CV / Résumé Best Practices: Evidence Review, Critique Rubric and Rewrite Rules

*Prepared for JobFerret (CV critique and tailoring). Research date: 2 November 2026. Default region UK; US, EU and AU conventions covered.*

**How to read the tags.** **[evidence]** means a linked study, dataset, official guidance or primary survey backs the claim. **[judgment]** means it is practitioner consensus or reasoning from evidence, and is not directly measured. **[folklore/debunked]** means it is widely repeated and false or unsupported. Sources that sell CV writing, templates or "ATS checkers" are marked **(vendor)**: not automatically wrong, but their incentive points one way.

## 1. TL;DR: the 20 rules that matter most (ranked)

| # | Rule | Confidence |
|---|------|-----------|
| 1 | **Never invent anything.** Reword, reorder and quantify only from facts the candidate supplied or confirmed. Every claim must survive an interview and a reference check. | High (ethics + detection risk) |
| 2 | **Write impact, not duties.** Each bullet shows an action, its result, and scale or evidence. Use Bock's "X, measured by Y, by doing Z" as a check, not a template. | High [judgment, strong consensus] |
| 3 | **Tailor every application.** Reorder by relevance to the job description (JD), use its exact terms for skills the candidate really has, and match the target title in the headline where honest. | High [evidence + judgment] |
| 4 | **Zero spelling or grammar errors.** In a 445-recruiter experiment, a few errors cut interview probability by about 7 points and many by about 18.5. | High [evidence] |
| 5 | **Make the top third of page one count.** The first skim checks titles, employers, dates and headline. | High [evidence, weak method] |
| 6 | **Single-column, text-based layout.** No layout tables, text boxes, header/footer contact details, images of text or skill graphics. | High [evidence + vendor consensus] |
| 7 | **Standard section names**: Profile, Experience, Education, Skills, Certifications, Projects. | High [judgment] |
| 8 | **Reverse-chronological or hybrid, never functional.** Functional CVs hide dates and read as concealment. | High [judgment, consensus] |
| 9 | **Length follows region and seniority.** UK 2 pages, AU 2–3; US 1 page early career, 2 at 10+ years; executive 2–3; academic as long as the record. Two pages is not penalised when earned. | Medium-high [vendor study + guidance] |
| 10 | **Follow the posting's file-format instruction.** Otherwise send a text-based PDF from Word or Docs, with a DOCX twin for Taleo, legacy Workday and agencies. | Medium-high [judgment] |
| 11 | **Respect regional personal-details norms.** UK/US/AU/IE: no photo, DOB, marital status, nationality or full address. DACH/France: photo still common, so ask. | High UK/US; medium DACH/FR |
| 12 | **Kill clichés and self-assessed soft skills.** Show traits through outcomes. | High [judgment; frequency data] |
| 13 | **Bullets of 1–2 lines; 3–6 for the most relevant role**, tapering to one line or less for roles 10–15+ years old. | Medium-high [judgment] |
| 14 | **Month-and-year dates, consistent and gap-honest.** Label any gap over 6 months in one line. | Medium-high [evidence on gap penalties] |
| 15 | **Hard skills grouped by category.** No rating bars, stars or percentages. | High [judgment] |
| 16 | **CV, LinkedIn and application form must match** on titles, employers and dates. Recruiters cross-check. | High [judgment; vendor experiment] |
| 17 | **No hidden keywords, white text or prompt injection.** Plain-text views expose them and recruiters look for them. | High [evidence; ethics] |
| 18 | **Remove AI tells**: uniform bullet rhythm, "spearheaded/leveraged", triplets, generic summary, US spelling on a UK CV. | Medium [vendor surveys, thin] |
| 19 | **UK public sector: the statement decides.** NHS and Civil Service shortlists usually turn on evidence against the person specification or behaviours. | High [official guidance] |
| 20 | **Proof the file, not just the text.** Extract the plain text, check reading order and contact details, file name, page count, and that no other company's name is left in. | High [judgment] |

## 2. Evidence review

### 2.1 How CVs are actually read

**The "6–7 second" figure.** The famous statistic comes from Ladders (a US job board that also sells CV services, so it is vendor-adjacent). The 2012 study tracked around 30 recruiters and reported about 6 seconds. The 2018 update reported an average "initial screen" of **7.4 seconds** ([HR Dive, 2018](https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/541582/)). The 2018 method had two stages. First, recruiters sorted CVs without knowing they were being timed. Then they were eye-tracked in a lab. Ladders has not published sample sizes, statistical method or how the averages were calculated, and critics have said so. **[evidence, weak methodology, 2012/2018: flag as old]**.

Even so, the qualitative findings are consistent and plausible:
- Recruiters fixated on **job titles**, then employers, dates and section headings.
- Top-performing CVs had simple layouts, clear headings and bolded titles.
- Poor performers had clutter, long sentences, multiple columns and little white space. **[evidence]**

**The "F-pattern"** is borrowed from Nielsen Norman's web-reading research. Ladders describes a scan down the left side and across headings but does not establish an "F". It is a metaphor, not a finding. **[judgment]**

**The 7 seconds is triage, not reading.** In ResumeGo's simulated hiring study, reviewers who were deciding between candidates spent **2 min 24 s on one-page CVs and 4 min 5 s on two-page CVs** ([ResumeGo, 2018](https://www.resumego.net/research/one-or-two-page-resumes/); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2018/12/19/resumego-hiring-managers-prefer-candidates-with-two-page-resumes.html)) **(vendor)**. The two numbers measure different tasks. A CV has to survive a seconds-long skim for fit, then a minutes-long read for evidence. **Design for both:** the top third of page one, plus the headings and titles, wins the skim, and the achievement bullets win the read. **[judgment, well supported]**

**What gets checked first** (recruiter consensus, consistent with the Ladders fixation data): current title and employer; whether the title progression points at the target role; dates and tenure; location and right to work; the headline or summary; must-have tools or qualifications in the skills section. **[judgment]**

**Who weighs what.**

| Reader | Primary question | What they weigh |
|---|---|---|
| ATS / parser | "What are the fields?" | Extracted contact details, titles, employers, dates, education, skills; knock-outs; increasingly an AI ranking layer. |
| Recruiter / TA partner | "Does this match the brief well enough to put forward?" | Title and industry match, must-have keywords, tenure, eligibility, red flags. Fast, pattern-based. |
| Hiring manager | "Can this person do *my* problems, and better than the others?" | Specific achievements, scope, depth, relevant context. Reads slowly and sceptically. |
| Panel (public sector) | "Does the evidence meet each criterion?" | Scores against the person specification or behaviours; often reads the statement first. |

**Volume context for 2025–26.** Greenhouse reports roughly **three times as many applicants per recruiter** as before, driven partly by AI-assisted applying. 22% of US job seekers say they have used an AI agent to apply ([HR Brew on Greenhouse, May 2025](https://hr-brew.com/stories/2025/05/14/greenhouse-unveils-new-ai-hiring-tools); [Fortune, Apr 2025](https://www.fortune.com/2025/04/09/greenhouse-ceo-daniel-chait-job-recruiting-ai-arms-race-ghost-jobs)). **(ATS vendor)** **[evidence]** More volume means more keyword search, more knock-outs and faster skims, so tailoring matters more than five years ago. **[judgment]**

**Skills-first search.** LinkedIn says recruiters are about **25% more likely to search by skills** than three years earlier, and more than half search by skills more than by years of experience ([LinkedIn Future of Recruiting 2025](https://business.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/business/en-us/talent-solutions/resources/pdfs/future-of-recruiting-2025-stff.pdf)) **(platform vendor)**. **[evidence]** So the exact skill names have to appear in text a parser can read.

### 2.2 ATS: how it really works vs the myths

**The pipeline.** A parser (often licensed from Textkernel/Sovren, Daxtra or RChilli) turns the uploaded file into text and fields. That becomes a structured profile alongside the application-form answers. Recruiters then search, filter and review the queue. **Knock-out questions** (right to work, licence, minimum years) can auto-disqualify. **[evidence/judgment]** Usage is near-universal at large employers. Jobscan detected an ATS at **97.8% of Fortune 500 companies in 2025**, with **Workday at 39.2%** and SAP SuccessFactors at 13.2% ([Jobscan](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/fortune-500-use-applicant-tracking-systems/)) **(vendor: Jobscan sells ATS-optimisation)**.

**Most ATS do not auto-reject on a "match score".** Enhancv interviewed 25 US recruiters. **23 of 25 (92%)** said their system did not auto-reject for formatting, content or design. The two exceptions used content thresholds in high-volume entry-level hiring. **All 25 used knock-out questions where present** ([Enhancv](https://enhancv.com/blog/does-ats-reject-resumes/)) **(vendor, tiny sample)**. Recruiter-side writers make the same point: people and filters reject candidates, not the parser ([HiringThing](https://blog.hiringthing.com/applicant-tracking-system-myths), an ATS vendor). **[evidence, thin but consistent]**

**Where "75% rejected by ATS" came from.** It traces to **Preptel**, a CV-optimisation software firm, around 2012. Preptel published no methodology and shut down in 2013. Fortune repeated it in 2014 ([Fortune](https://dc.fortune.com/2014/08/22/how-to-keep-your-resume-from-getting-lost-in-cyberspace/)); a fact-check traces its spread to circular citation ([Lenz](https://lenz.io/c/ats-applicant-tracking-system-75-percent-rejection-rate-bc6fd7d4)). **[folklore/debunked]**

**But 2024–26 adds a real caveat. AI ranking is not a myth.**
- Harvard Business School and Accenture's *Hidden Workers* report (2021) found **88% of employers agreed** that qualified high-skill candidates are screened out because they don't match the JD's exact criteria. ATS filter configuration was a leading cause ([HBS report PDF](https://www.hbs.edu/managing-the-future-of-work/Documents/research/hiddenworkers09032021.pdf); [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/report-applicant-tracking-systems-may-exclude-whole-segments-of-candidates/606614/)). That is humans filtering with rigid criteria, not autonomous rejection, but the candidate sees the same result. **[evidence]**
- Resume Builder polled 948 business leaders in October 2024. **21%** said AI may reject candidates at every stage without human review, and a further **50%** allow it at the initial CV screen ([Resume Builder](https://resumebuilder.com/7-in-10-companies-will-use-ai-in-the-hiring-process-in-2025-despite-most-saying-its-biased/)) **(vendor; online panel; treat as directional)**.
- In ***Mobley v. Workday***, a US federal court in May 2025 granted preliminary certification of a nationwide age-discrimination collective action. The suit is over AI features that "score, sort, rank, or screen" applicants ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/05/federal-court-allows-collective-action-lawsuit-over-alleged)). Not a merits ruling, but proof AI ranking is deployed at scale. **[evidence]**

**Net position for the agent:** "The ATS auto-rejects you for a fancy font" is folklore. "Your CV is searched, filtered, knocked out and increasingly *ranked* by software before a human reads it closely" is true. The practical advice barely changes: parse cleanly, state the exact relevant terms, and answer knock-outs truthfully. **[judgment]**

**What breaks parsing.** Textkernel says **at least 15% of CVs use column layouts**, and older parsers would "mix bits and pieces of each column together" ([Textkernel](https://textkernel.com/learn-support/blog/improving-extraction-from-column-resumes)). Modern parsers detect columns better, but the underlying failure is the **reading order of the text layer**, not columns as such. **[evidence]** Other common failures, by vendor and recruiter consensus: layout tables, text boxes, contact details in the Word header or footer, icons in place of labels, images of text or outlined design-tool exports, unusual section names ("My Journey"), decorative fonts, inconsistent date formats, and skill bars. **[judgment, broadly corroborated]**

**DOCX vs PDF in 2025–26.** Modern parsers handle **text-based PDFs** well. Older Taleo and some Workday configurations are reported to map DOCX more reliably, but that evidence is anecdotal and **thin** ([recruiter blog](https://thebiggamehunter.us/workday-and-pdfs/)). Rule: follow the posting's instruction. Otherwise send a PDF exported from Word or Google Docs. Switch to the DOCX twin if autofill garbles fields or the portal is Taleo or legacy Workday. Never upload a design-tool PDF without a text-extraction check. **[judgment]** "ATS can't read PDFs" is **[folklore]**.

**Keyword matching.** Recruiter search is mostly literal or Boolean: "PM" won't reliably match "Product Manager". Write the acronym and the full form once each ("Search Engine Optimisation (SEO)"). Mirror the JD's exact form for tools really used (PostgreSQL, not Postgres). Put keywords in the headline, the skills block *and* the bullets that prove them. A keyword found only in a skills list is weak with a human. **[judgment]**

Jobscan's 2025 survey of 442 job seekers reports that people who matched the posting's job title had a **10.6× interview rate** ([Jobscan](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/interview-rates-study)). This is **(vendor, self-reported, confounded)**, because people who match titles apply to better-fitting jobs. The direction is plausible, the magnitude is not credible.

**What third-party "ATS score" tools measure.** Jobscan, Resume Worded and similar tools compute **keyword overlap** with the JD plus formatting heuristics. They are **not** the employer's ATS and don't reproduce its ranking. A 90% "match" from pasting in the JD is worse than a 65% match that reads well. Use them only to find keyword gaps. **[judgment]**

**White-text and prompt-injection stuffing.** Parsing strips colour, so hidden text shows in plain view ([Hiration](https://www.hiration.com/blog/white-text-resume-hack/), vendor). Greenhouse's 2025 research says **41% of US job seekers admit using prompt injections** and **65% of US hiring managers have caught deceptive AI use** ([Greenhouse, 2025](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/an-ai-trust-crisis-70-of-hiring-managers-trust-ai-to-make-faster-and-better-hiring-decisions-only-8-of-job-seekers-call-it-fair)) **(vendor)**. It is detectable, dishonest and grounds for blacklisting. **[evidence + ethics]**

**"Use the ATS-friendly template"** is mostly marketing. Any clean single-column document with real text and standard headings is "ATS-friendly". Paid templates add nothing a parser rewards. **[judgment]**

### 2.3 Structure and format

**Length.**
- **ResumeGo (2018)**: 482 recruiters, hiring managers and HR staff made 7,712 simulated choices between matched one- and two-page CVs. They chose the two-page version **2.3×** as often, or **1.4×** for entry-level roles ([ResumeGo](https://www.resumego.net/research/one-or-two-page-resumes/)). **Critiques:**
  - It is a simulation, not a field experiment, despite being reported as one.
  - The two-page versions contained *more content*, so length and information are confounded.
  - ResumeGo sells CV writing.
  - It is US-centric and 8 years old.
- **What it does establish:** the US "one page or nothing" rule is **[folklore]** for experienced candidates. Two pages are not penalised when the content earns them. **[evidence, moderate]**
- **UK:** university careers services and councils converge on **2 pages maximum, 1 acceptable early career, 3 for senior or academic** ([Birmingham City Council, updated Nov 2025](https://www.birmingham.gov.uk/info/50160/employment_advice/1508/cv_writing/5); [University of Manchester](https://www.careers.manchester.ac.uk/services/cv/cvfaqs/)).
- **US:** 1 page for students and roughly the first 5–7 years. 2 pages for 10+ years. Federal (USAJOBS) résumés are longer and highly detailed.
- **AU:** typically **2–3 pages**, and referees are commonly listed ([University of Queensland](https://employability.uq.edu.au/files/176803/Resume%20Fact%20Sheet%20%26%20Example.pdf)).
- **Academic:** full record. **Executive:** 2–3 pages. **[evidence/judgment]**

**Section order (experienced candidates):** name and contact line → headline and 3–4 line profile → optional core-skills block → Experience → Education → Certifications → Projects, publications or volunteering if relevant → optional interests. **Graduates:** Education first, then Experience, Projects, Skills. **Career changers:** a relevant-projects or training section can sit above Experience. **[judgment]**

**Format choice.** Reverse-chronological is the default. Hybrid (profile and skills up top, then chronological history) suits career changers and returners. **Functional** (skill clusters without dates or employers) reads as concealment and parses badly, because there are no title–employer–date triplets. Treat it as a red flag. **[judgment, strong consensus]**

**Dates.** Month and year ("Mar 2022 – Present"), one format throughout. Years alone suggest hidden gaps, except for roles 15+ years old. Show overlaps honestly. A gap of more than about 6 months gets a one-line entry, e.g. "Career break — caring responsibilities (2023–2024); completed AWS Solutions Architect Associate". **[judgment]**

**Personal details by region.**

| Region | Include | Exclude / caution |
|---|---|---|
| **UK / IE** | Name, phone, email, town/city (or "London, open to hybrid"), LinkedIn URL, portfolio/GitHub if relevant. Right to work if it may be in doubt ("Full UK right to work, no sponsorship required"). | Photo, DOB/age, marital status, nationality (unless as right to work), full postal address, NI number, gender, religion, health ([University of Kent](https://student.kent.ac.uk/careers/making-applications/cv)). |
| **US / CA** | Name, phone, email, city/state, LinkedIn. Work authorisation if relevant. | Photo (EEO risk, and many employers discard it), DOB, SSN, marital status. |
| **AU / NZ** | As UK. Referees (2–3) are commonly listed. Visa status if not a citizen or permanent resident. | Photo, DOB. |
| **DACH** | Photo still common and often expected, though not legally required. The anti-discrimination law (AGG) means employers cannot demand it ([law-school.de](https://www.law-school.de/master/article/job-application-in-germany-the-photo-issue/); [karriere.at](https://www.karriere.at/c/a/foto-im-lebenslauf)). DOB and nationality often included; tabular *Lebenslauf* in traditional sectors. | **Ask.** International firms and startups increasingly accept no photo. |
| **France / Benelux / S. Europe** | Photo and age are optional but still common in France ([VisualCV, vendor](https://visualcv.com/international/france-cv)). | Ask the user. Anonymous-CV options exist (France Travail). |
| **EU institutions / some public employers** | **Europass** format, when requested ([Europass](https://europass.europa.eu/en)). | Not for private-sector UK/US roles; it reads as bureaucratic. |

Photos are not neutral. In an Israeli correspondence study of about 5,300 CVs, attractive men gained callbacks, while women with *no* photo did better than attractive or plain women ([Ruffle & Shtudiner, *Management Science* 2015](https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/are-good-looking-people-more-employable/)). **[evidence]** Default to no photo unless local norms say otherwise.

**Typography and layout.** **[judgment, consensus]**
- **Fonts and size:** Calibri, Arial, Helvetica, Lato, Source Sans or Georgia (Garamond only at 11.5pt+, as it runs small). 10.5–12pt body, 14–20pt name.
- **Page:** margins 1.8–2.5cm; below about 1.5cm looks crammed and risks print clipping. **A4 for UK/EU/AU, Letter for US/CA.**
- **Emphasis:** bold titles and employers only, plus at most one or two phrases per section. One restrained accent colour at most. Same date format, bullet glyph and spacing throughout.
- **Bullets and tense:** 1–2 lines each. Past tense for past roles and completed achievements, present tense for current duties. No "I" or "my" in bullets.
- **File name:** `Firstname-Lastname-CV.pdf`, optionally with the role (`Jane-Smith-CV-Product-Manager.pdf`). Never `CV_final_v3_ACME.docx`.
- **References:** drop "available on request" in the UK and US, where it is assumed. In AU, list referees or use the line ([UQ](https://employability.uq.edu.au/files/176803/Resume%20Fact%20Sheet%20%26%20Example.pdf)).
- **Interests:** only if specific and relevant or a trait signal ("Ultra-marathons (5 × 100km)", "Maintainer of an open-source Rust crate, 2k stars"). Drop "reading, travel, socialising".

### 2.4 Content that converts

**Profile or summary** (3–4 lines, or 50–80 words, role-targeted). Formula: *[Target title] with [N years] in [domain/context], known for [1–2 signature outcomes with numbers]. [Key tools or qualifications]. [What you're looking for or bring, optional].* **[judgment]**

- *Bad:* "Results-driven, highly motivated professional with a passion for excellence and a proven track record of success. Excellent communicator and team player seeking a challenging role."
- *Good (UK):* "Product manager with 6 years in B2B SaaS payments, most recently owning onboarding at a 40k-merchant platform, where activation rose from 31% to 46% in a year. Strong in discovery, experimentation (Amplitude, Optimizely) and working with regulated finance partners. Looking for a senior PM role on growth or platform."

Objectives ("Seeking a role where I can grow…") are **dated**. Replace them with a profile. **[judgment]**

**Achievement bullets.** Laszlo Bock (then SVP People at Google) proposed "**Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y], by doing [Z]**" in 2014 ([Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.in/google-hr-boss-says-this-is-the-key-to-a-perfect-resume/articleshow/43924179.cms); [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/bill-murphy-jr/google-recruiters-say-these-5-resume-tips-including-x-y-z-formula-will-improve-your-odds-of-getting-hired-at-google.html)). **[evidence of origin; 2014, flag as old]** CAR (Challenge–Action–Result), PAR and STAR are equivalent. STAR belongs in interviews and supporting statements, CAR and XYZ belong on CVs. Use them as **completeness checks**. If every bullet is the same template, the CV reads as machine-made. Vary the order: result-first for wins, action-first for scope. **[judgment]**

**Quantifying when the candidate "has no numbers."** Ask targeted questions, then use whichever is true:
- **Scale/scope:** people, users, budget, sites, accounts, patients per shift, countries.
- **Frequency/volume:** "~60 calls/day", "weekly reporting to the exec team".
- **Before → after:** time, cost, error rate, backlog, complaints. Honest approximations are fine ("roughly halved").
- **Rank/recognition:** "top 3 of 22 advisers", promotion, chosen to train others.
- **Proxy outcomes and delivery:** "process adopted by 4 other teams", "delivered in 6 weeks against a 10-week plan".

If no number exists, a **concrete specific** beats an invented number: "Rewrote the onboarding guide; new starters now shadow for 1 week instead of 3." **[judgment]**

**Duties vs impact.** "Responsible for managing social media accounts" describes the job; "Grew LinkedIn followers 4.1k → 11k in 12 months with a weekly employee-story series" describes the person. Everyone with the title had the first. **[judgment, consensus]**

**Bullets per role.** Current or most relevant role: **3–6**. The previous one or two roles: **2–4**. Roles 10+ years old: **0–2**, or a single "Earlier career" line listing titles and employers. **[judgment]**

**Tailoring (per application).** Extract the JD's must-haves, exact title and domain terms. Map each must-have to evidence, and mark it **gap** if there is none (never fabricate). Retarget the headline where experience genuinely matches, leaving official titles intact. Reorder bullets and skills so the JD-relevant ones lead, weave exact JD terms into existing true bullets, and cut irrelevant ones. The HBS *Hidden Workers* finding is why: rigid exact-criteria matching is how employers say qualified people get missed. **[evidence + judgment]**

**Skills section.** Group **hard skills** by category ("Languages: Python, SQL, TypeScript | Cloud: AWS (Lambda, S3), Terraform"). Add proficiency only where it means something ("Spanish (C1)"). **No soft-skill lists**, which are unverifiable, and **no rating bars, stars or percentages**, which don't parse ("Python 80%" means nothing). **[judgment]**

**Education.** First for students, recent graduates (roughly 0–3 years) and academics, otherwise after Experience. UK: degree class, institution and years; A-levels early-career only; GCSEs only if asked. US: GPA if 3.5+ and early career. Dropping graduation years for senior candidates is legitimate if done consistently. **[judgment]**

**Certifications, projects, volunteering.** Regulatory or gating certifications get their own section, with an ID or registration number where checkable and "(expected Mar 2027)" for in-progress ones. Projects are essential for tech, data, design, early-career and career-change candidates; write them like jobs (name, link, stack, outcome). Substantial, relevant volunteering goes under Experience. **[judgment]**

**Hard cases.**
- **Gaps.** Callbacks for "opted-out" parents in a US audit study were dramatically lower than for continuously employed applicants, and lower than for unemployed ones ([Weisshaar, *ASR* 2018](https://doi.org/10.1177/0003122417752355)). **[evidence, US, 2018]** LinkedIn's 2022 survey (about 23k workers, 4k hiring managers) found **1 in 5 hiring managers reject career-break candidates outright**, but more than half are more likely to call back when context is given ([Quartz](https://qz.com/work/2136597/linkedin-now-lets-you-explain-a-break-in-your-work-history); [CNBC 2023](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/10/career-breaks-and-4-tips-for-explaining-resume-gaps-workplace-experts.html)). **[evidence, vendor survey]** **Rule:** label the gap in one neutral line, add any skills kept current, never fake an employer, and never stretch dates.
- **Redundancy.** Don't put it on the CV. The cover letter or interview handles it ("role made redundant in restructure"). It carries little stigma in 2025–26 tech and finance. **[judgment]**
- **Returners.** Hybrid format, a "Career break" entry, recent courses or freelance work; target returnships. **[judgment]**
- **Visa status.** State it where it helps ("No sponsorship required"; "Graduate visa valid to 08/2027"). Silence makes recruiters guess. **[judgment]**
- **Salary.** Never on the CV. Put it in the form field if mandatory. **[judgment, consensus]**
- **Career changers.** Hybrid format. A headline naming the *target* field, honestly ("Teacher moving into L&D / instructional design"). A "Relevant experience & projects" section first. Translate the old domain's terms into the new one's ("differentiated instruction for 30 learners" becomes "designed learning for mixed-ability cohorts of 30"). Drop old-field jargon. **[judgment]**
- **Senior applying down.** Keep titles but trim scope language, emphasise hands-on delivery, and explain the motive in the cover letter. **[judgment]**

**The honesty line.** Emphasise, select, reorder, reframe and translate, but never invent or inflate. Reference calls, background checks and technical interviews expose fabrication, and in the UK it can be fraud by false representation (Fraud Act 2006). The precise rule is in §4.1. **[judgment + legal]**

### 2.5 Language

**Clichés to kill** (replace each with evidence): results-driven, proven track record, dynamic, passionate, team player, hard-working, detail-oriented, self-starter, go-getter, think outside the box, synergy, excellent communication skills. Duty phrases too: responsible for, duties included, tasked with, helped with, worked on, involved in.

Careerminds' 2025 analysis found "dynamic" used by 19,633 job seekers in a year, "dedicated" by 16,316 and "proven ability" by 15,903 ([Careerminds](https://careerminds.com/blog/the-most-overused-words-oovn-resumes-and-which-lead-to-a-hire)) **(vendor)**. These words don't differentiate. **[evidence of frequency; judgment on effect]**

**Weak verbs → accurate, stronger verbs.** "Worked on" becomes built, analysed, drafted or migrated. "Handled" becomes resolved or negotiated. "Was responsible for" becomes ran, owned or managed. "Helped" becomes "co-delivered" or "supported X to [result]". The verb must match reality: "led" requires leading. **Passive voice** hides who acted; use active voice, though "Promoted to X within 14 months" is a fine exception. **[judgment]**

**Buzzword density check.** If more than about one bullet in five contains an abstract noun stack ("cross-functional strategic stakeholder alignment initiatives"), rewrite. **[judgment]**

**AI-generated tells (CV-specific).** In TopResume's May 2025 Pollfish survey of 600 US hiring managers, **19.6% said they would reject** a candidate for an AI-generated CV or cover letter, and about a third said they can spot one ([TopResume](https://topresume.com/career-advice/ai-in-hiring-survey)) **(vendor)**. The tells recruiters and editors cite: every bullet the same shape (verb + noun phrase + "resulting in X%"); "spearheaded / leveraged / orchestrated / championed / fostered" throughout; triplets ("strategy, execution, and optimisation"); a summary that could be anyone's; suspiciously round, uniform percentages; US spelling on a UK CV; "showcasing my ability to"; and no concrete nouns (products, customers, systems, places).

**Evidence is thin** that recruiters reliably detect AI text, but generic writing fails for reasons that have nothing to do with AI. **[judgment]**

**The AI-writing paradox (and why it matters to this agent).**
- **Positive evidence.** In a field experiment with nearly 500k job seekers, *non-generative* writing assistance (error correction and clarity) raised hiring by **8%**. Treated CVs had fewer errors and were more readable, and employers showed no later dissatisfaction ([Wiles, Munyikwa & Horton, NBER w30886 / *Management Science* 2025](https://www.nber.org/papers/w30886)). **[evidence]**
- **Negative evidence.** LLM evaluators favour CVs written by the same LLM over equally qualified human-written ones. In simulated pipelines, that gave a **23–60% shortlisting advantage** ([Xu, Li & Jiang, AIES 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00462)). **[evidence, simulation]**
- **Policy:** use AI to *edit*: errors, clarity, concision, keyword coverage. Keep the content *specific and human*: real nouns, numbers and context. Don't chase AI-screener bias with LLM-flavoured prose: the human makes the final call, and the bias is model-specific. **[judgment]**

**UK vs US spelling and terminology.**

| | UK | US |
|---|---|---|
| Document name | CV | résumé/resume (CV = academic) |
| Spelling | organised, optimised, programme (but "computer program"), centre, licence (noun), analyse, modelling, colour | organized, optimized, program, center, license, analyze, modeling, color |
| Grades | First, 2:1, 2:2; A-levels (A*AA); GCSEs (9–4 / A*–C) | GPA (3.7/4.0), Dean's List, magna cum laude |
| Education words | university, sixth form, placement year / sandwich | college, school, internship / co-op |

Pick one variety and run a consistency check. **Mixed spelling is a credibility tell.** **[judgment]**

### 2.6 Sector and seniority modifiers (short notes; full table in §6)

These notes add only what the §6 table can't carry. **[judgment]** unless linked.

- **Tech / data / AI:** list only technologies the candidate would be happy to be grilled on, not 40 of them. Distinguish "built" from "used". Data and AI bullets must name the business decision the work drove, not just the model.
- **Design / creative:** a visual CV is fine for direct email or a portfolio, but always **keep an ATS-safe single-column twin** for portals.
- **Finance / legal:** the zero-error bar is higher here. Senior deal or matter lists can go in an annex.
- **NHS:** the application form plus the **supporting information**, written against the person specification, does most of the work ([RCN](https://rcn.org.uk/Professional-Development/Your-career/Job-applications); [GMMH NHS FT](https://gmmh.nhs.uk/tips-for-applying-and-interviewing)).
- **UK Civil Service:** behaviour examples are usually **250 words** each, and personal statements are commonly **750–1,250 words** as set per advert. Assessors **stop reading at the limit**, and the CV is often anonymised and sometimes sifted only after the behaviours are scored ([personal statement](https://www.civil-service-careers.gov.uk/how-to-write-your-ps); [CV guidance](https://www.civil-service-careers.gov.uk/how-to-write-your-cv); [Success Profiles](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/success-profiles/success-profiles-candidate-overview)). **[evidence]**
- **Academia:** funders increasingly impose narrative formats (UKRI's Résumé for Research and Innovation), which replace the CV for that application.
- **Executive:** put 3–5 headline achievements above the history.
- **Sales:** a sales CV without attainment numbers fails.

### 2.7 Red flags recruiters cite

| Red flag | Evidence | Agent action |
|---|---|---|
| **Typos / grammar** | 445 Flemish recruiters: 7.3pp lower interview probability with few errors, 18.5pp with many; bigger in writing-heavy jobs, as recruiters infer lower conscientiousness and ability ([Sterkens et al., *PLOS ONE* 2023](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0283280); [IZA DP 14614](https://iza.org/publications/dp/14614)). **[evidence]** | Zero tolerance; spell-check in the right locale. |

| **Inconsistent dates or formatting** | Reads as carelessness or concealment. **[judgment]** | Consistency pass. |

| **Lying / exaggeration** | Exposed by references, checks and technical interviews. **[judgment]** | Honesty rules (§4.1). |

| **Overlong, dense, irrelevant early-career detail** | Ladders: clutter and long sentences performed worst. **[evidence, weak]** | Cut 3+-line bullets; collapse old roles to "Earlier career". |

### 2.8 Companion documents (brief)

**LinkedIn alignment.**
- Titles, employers and dates must match the CV. Recruiters routinely open the profile, and mismatches look like fabrication.
- Headline: target title plus specialism plus a proof point, not "Seeking opportunities".
- About: a first-person, longer version of the profile (3 short paragraphs), with keywords.
- Experience: can carry more bullets than the CV.
- Skills: list the top 5 recruiters search for.
- Use LinkedIn's "Career break" type for gaps.
- ResumeGo's 2019 field experiment (about 24.5k fictitious applications) found CVs linking a **comprehensive** LinkedIn profile got a **13.5%** callback rate, against **7.9%** with no link and **7.2%** with a bare-bones profile ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2019/03/28/job-applicants-with-a-comprehensive-linkedin-profile-71-more-likely-to-get-interviews-study-says)) **(vendor, 2019)**. A thin profile is worse than none, so link it only if it is complete. **[evidence, moderate]**

**Cover letter.** Send one whenever it is required, or optional and the role matters, and always for career changes, relocations, gaps, senior roles and small companies. Vendor surveys say most hiring managers read them (Resume Genius: 83%) ([Resume Genius](https://resumegenius.com/blog/cover-letter-help/cover-letter-statistics)) **(vendor; self-report)**. Keep it to **250–400 words**:
1. A specific hook: why this company or role, tied to something real (a product, news, a problem in the JD).
2. Two short paragraphs mapping the best 2–3 achievements to the JD's top needs.
3. Anything the CV can't say: relocation, a gap, a career change, right to work.
4. A plain close with an ask: "I'd welcome a conversation about how I could help with X."

Don't open with "I am writing to apply for…", which wastes the most-read line. Try: "Your Head of Data post asks for someone who has rebuilt a reporting stack under regulatory scrutiny — I did that at Monzo in 2024." Address a named person where findable. **[judgment]**

**UK supporting statements (NHS / Civil Service / HE / local government).** Structure the statement **by the person specification's criteria, in order**, using their wording as subheadings. Give one STAR example per criterion, with your personal role ("I", not "we") and the result. Cover every essential criterion and as many desirables as possible. Respect word limits exactly. Civil Service behaviours are assessed **at the grade level**, so pitch examples to the grade ([Success Profiles behaviours](https://www.civil-service-careers.gov.uk/success-profiles/behaviours)). **[evidence, official]**

## 3. CV Critique Rubric

Score each dimension from 1 to 5. Report the scores, the **three highest-leverage fixes**, and then the detail. Weighting for an overall score (out of 100): Impact 20, Relevance 15, Credibility 15, ATS 10, Structure 10, Clarity 10, Language 8, Visual 5, Region/sector 4, Red flags 3. **Any critical red flag**, such as fabrication, a wrong company name or hidden text, **caps the overall score at 40** until it is fixed. Feedback must be direct: say what is wrong, why it costs interviews, and what to do. Do not pad it with praise.

### 3.1 Impact and achievement density
- **Checks:** in the last 2–3 roles, the share of bullets with an outcome (number, before→after, scale, rank or recognition); the share starting with duty phrases ("responsible for", "assisted"); whether the strongest achievement sits in the top third of page 1.
- **Anchors:** 1 = pure duties, no outcomes. 2 = under 25% show outcomes, mostly vague. 3 = 40–60% outcomes, some vague ("improved efficiency"). 4 = over 60% specific outcomes, the best near the top. 5 = nearly every bullet shows specific, credible impact, and the reader instantly knows what this person is great at.
- **Example feedback:** "Of your 14 bullets at Acme, 11 describe the job, not what you achieved in it. Anyone with your title could have written them. Rewrite at least the top 4 with a result. I've drafted questions below to pull the numbers out of you."

### 3.2 Relevance and tailoring to the target role
- **Checks:** build a coverage matrix of JD must-haves (evidenced in a bullet / listed only / absent). Does the headline name the target role? Are the most relevant bullets first in each role? Is irrelevant content taking space?
- **Anchors:** 1 = generic, target role can't be inferred. 2 = overlap by accident. 3 = key skills present but buried or only in the skills list. 4 = most must-haves evidenced, sensibly ordered. 5 = every must-have the candidate genuinely has is evidenced early, in the JD's own terms, with gaps handled honestly.
- **Example feedback:** "The JD's top three needs are stakeholder management with clinicians, SQL, and NHS data standards. Your CV mentions SQL once, in the skills list, and the other two not at all, though your bullet about the A&E dashboard is exactly that experience. Move it to the top and name the data standard you used."

### 3.3 ATS parse-ability
- **Checks:** extract the plain text from the actual file (e.g. `pdftotext -layout` or python-docx) and confirm the reading order. Contact details must be in the body, not a header or footer. No tables, text boxes or columns holding core content; no icons in place of labels; no images of text. Standard headings, consistent parseable dates, acronym plus full form for key terms, selectable text, file type as instructed.
- **Anchors:** 1 = image or scanned file, or extraction yields garbage. 2 = columns, tables or text boxes scramble the order, or contact details are lost. 3 = minor risks (icons, odd headings, contact in the header). 4 = clean single column, cosmetic risks only. 5 = extracts perfectly with every key field present.
- **Example feedback:** "When I extract the text from your PDF, the right-hand column (skills and contact details) lands in the middle of your second job. A parser will do the same. Move to one column and put your email and phone in the body, not the page header."

### 3.4 Structure and scannability
- **Checks:** can you find the current title, employer and dates in under 5 seconds? Reverse-chronological or hybrid, not functional? Section order right for the career stage? Bullets tapered toward older roles? Length right for region and seniority?
- **Anchors:** 1 = functional or chaotic, titles and dates hard to find. 2 = chronological but cluttered. 3 = readable, but the top third is wasted (objective, long address, skills cloud). 4 = clear hierarchy, the top third earns its space. 5 = a 7-second skim gives the correct picture of who this is and why they fit.
- **Example feedback:** "Your first half-page is an address block, an objective and a list of 20 soft skills. The recruiter's first glance finds nothing about what you've done. Put a 3-line profile and your current role there."

### 3.5 Clarity and concision
- **Checks:** bullets over 2 lines; sentences over about 30 words; unexplained jargon; repetition across roles; total words (roughly 450–800 for 2 pages).
- **Anchors:** 1 = dense paragraphs. 2 = many 3–4-line bullets, repetition. 3 = mostly tight, some sprawl. 4 = tight, rare long bullets. 5 = nothing could be cut without loss.
- **Example feedback:** "Seven of your bullets run to three lines or more. Cut each to its result and the one action that produced it. The bit about 'working closely with a range of internal and external stakeholders' can go in all seven."

### 3.6 Credibility and consistency
- **Checks:** dates consistent internally and with LinkedIn; overlaps explained; metrics plausible (all round numbers? 300% everywhere?); scope consistent with title (a junior with a "£50m P&L"); verbs match the role ("led" by an intern); certifications verifiable; gaps labelled.
- **Anchors:** 1 = contradictions or implausible claims. 2 = inflated verbs, unexplained gaps. 3 = plausible, some claims unsupported. 4 = consistent and specific. 5 = every claim would survive a reference call.
- **Example feedback:** "You say you 'led' the migration as a graduate analyst, and one bullet claims a 400% efficiency gain. A hiring manager will discount both, and doubt the rest. Say what *you* did in the migration, and give the before/after figure behind the 400%."

### 3.7 Visual formatting
- **Checks:** font and size in range; margins of 1.8cm or more; consistent date alignment, bullets, spacing and bold; paper size matches region (A4/Letter); no orphan lines on page 2; restrained colour.
- **Anchors:** 1 = inconsistent, cramped or distractingly decorative. 2 = several inconsistencies. 3 = clean but plain or slightly cramped. 4 = consistent and professional. 5 = balanced white space and a hierarchy that guides the eye.
- **Example feedback:** "Your dates are right-aligned in two roles and inline in three, and you use three bullet styles. It looks unedited. Pick one of each."

### 3.8 Language quality
- **Checks:** spelling and grammar in the correct locale; UK/US consistency; counts of clichés, duty phrases, passive constructions and first-person pronouns; tense consistency; AI tells (repeated "spearheaded/leveraged", uniform bullet rhythm, triplets, generic summary).
- **Anchors:** 1 = errors present. 2 = error-free but cliché-heavy or full of AI tells. 3 = clean, some stock phrasing. 4 = clean, active, specific. 5 = precise, varied and unmistakably about this person.
- **Example feedback:** "There's a typo in your profile ('stakeholder managment'). That alone measurably cuts interview odds. You also use 'spearheaded' four times and 'leveraged' three times. Recruiters now read that as ChatGPT. Use the plain verb for what you did."

### 3.9 Region and sector fit
- **Checks:** personal details, spelling variety, length, paper size and grade terms correct for the region; sector must-haves present (registration numbers, tickets, quota figures, publications, portfolio); right to work stated where relevant.
- **Anchors:** 1 = wrong conventions throughout (US résumé with photo and DOB for a UK NHS job). 2 = several mismatches. 3 = minor mismatches. 4 = right conventions. 5 = right conventions, with the sector's credibility markers prominent.
- **Example feedback:** "This is formatted as a US résumé (Letter size, GPA, 'organized') for a UK role. Switch to A4 and UK spelling, give your 2:1, and add your NMC PIN under your name. It's the first thing the ward manager will look for."

### 3.10 Red-flag check (pass/fail; any fail is listed first in feedback)
- **Checks:** wrong company or role name from a previous application; fabricated or unverifiable claims; hidden text or prompt injection; photo, DOB, marital status or NI number (UK/US); salary or references; unprofessional email; skill bars; functional format hiding dates; unexplained gap of 6+ months; typos; file name.
- **Anchors:** 1 = any critical failure. 3 = minor items only. 5 = clean.
- **Example feedback:** "Stop. Your profile says 'excited to join Deloitte'. This application is for PwC. Fix this before anything else."

## 4. Rewrite rules (prioritised)

### 4.1 The honesty boundary (non-negotiable)

**The agent MAY:**
1. **Reword** for clarity, concision, active voice and stronger *accurate* verbs.
2. **Reorder** bullets, sections and skills for relevance.
3. **Cut** material that doesn't serve the target role.
4. **Translate terminology** to the employer's vocabulary *when the underlying activity is the same*. ("service users" → "customers").
5. **Use the JD's exact skill names** for skills actually used (PostgreSQL only if they used PostgreSQL).
6. **Add a headline** using the target title when the candidate's experience substantively matches it. Example: "Data Analyst" for someone whose official title was "Insights Executive" but who did SQL and dashboarding.
7. **Clarify an official title** in brackets: "Associate II (Data Analyst)". Use this only when the bracketed title is an accurate functional description that a reference would agree with.
8. **Quantify** using numbers the candidate supplied or confirmed, or arithmetic derived from them. Approximations must be marked as such ("~", "roughly") and confirmed by the user.
9. **Ask questions** to surface numbers, scope and outcomes. This is the main lever for impact.
10. **Frame gaps, redundancy and career changes** positively and truthfully.

**The agent MUST NOT:**
1. Invent or alter employers, titles, dates, degrees, grades, certifications, licences, publications or awards.
2. Invent metrics, team sizes, budgets, user counts or percentages, or "round up" a confirmed figure beyond normal rounding.
3. Add a tool, skill or methodology to the CV that the candidate hasn't confirmed using. Do not move "exposure to" or "familiar with" up to "expert" or "led".
4. Upgrade the candidate's role in a team outcome ("contributed to" → "led") without confirmation.
5. Claim a JD requirement the candidate lacks. Report it as a gap, and suggest how to address it in the cover letter or by upskilling.
6. Stretch dates to hide gaps, or merge separate roles to hide short tenures (grouping agency or contract work transparently is fine).
7. Insert hidden text, white text, prompt-injection strings or keyword lists unrelated to experience.
8. Remove a qualification or date in a way that creates a false impression. Omitting graduation years for age reasons is acceptable. Omitting an employer in a way that creates a false impression of continuity is not.

**The test:** *Could the candidate say this sentence aloud, unprompted, to the hiring manager, with their former line manager in the room, and stand behind it?* If not, it doesn't go in.

**Provenance:** every changed claim must trace to (a) the master CV, (b) a user answer in this session, or (c) the user's stored profile. Keep a change log listing each new number or claim with its source. Mark unconfirmed items as `[CONFIRM: …]` and never ship them unresolved.

### 4.2 Do (in priority order)
1. Fix critical red flags first: a wrong company, errors, hidden text, photo/DOB (UK/US), salary.
2. Ensure clean parsing: single column, body-text contact details, standard headings, text-based file. Run a text-extraction check.
3. Write a role-specific headline and a 3–4 line profile with 1–2 proof points.
4. Convert duty bullets to achievement bullets (XYZ/CAR), leading with the most JD-relevant.
5. Interview the user for numbers: scale, frequency, before→after, rank, recognition.
6. Mirror the JD's exact terms for genuinely held skills, once in the skills block and once inside an evidencing bullet. Write the acronym and full form once each.
7. Taper detail with age: 3–6 bullets for the current or most relevant role, 2–4 for the next, an "Earlier career" line for 10–15+ years ago.
8. Group hard skills by category. Delete soft-skill lists and rating graphics.
9. Apply regional conventions: spelling, paper size, personal details, grade terms, referees (AU).
10. Apply sector modifiers (§6).
11. Run a consistency pass: date format, tense, punctuation (end bullets with or without full stops, consistently), bold usage, and LinkedIn alignment.
12. Vary bullet rhythm and verbs. Cap any single "power verb" at 1 use. Prefer plain verbs (built, cut, ran, wrote, sold, fixed).
13. Name the file `Firstname-Lastname-CV[-Role].pdf`. Produce a DOCX twin.
14. Produce a **change summary** for the user: what changed and why, plus the open `[CONFIRM]` items.

### 4.3 Don't
- Don't use clichés (§2.5 list), duty phrases, first-person pronouns in bullets, or objectives.
- Don't make every bullet "Verb + X, resulting in Y% improvement". It reads as templated.
- Don't use the AI verb set (spearheaded, leveraged, orchestrated, championed, fostered, synergised, delved) or triplets. Ration dashes used as punctuation to one per document: the "em dash = AI" belief is largely a meme about 2023–24 ChatGPT output (see `04-human-voice-writing.md`), but recruiters have heard it.
- Don't use tables, text boxes, columns, header/footer contact details, icons, images, skill bars, or more than 2 font families.
- Don't keyword-stuff, or paste the JD's responsibilities as bullets.
- Don't over-bold. Bold has no ATS value, and bolding every technology destroys the visual hierarchy.
- Don't pad to two pages or cram to one.
- Don't change official job titles (see the bracket rule), or hide gaps with year-only dates.

## 5. Bullet transformations (before → after)

*All numbers in the "after" versions are illustrative. The agent must get them from the user, and they must be true.*

1. **Software engineering (backend)**
   - *Before:* "Responsible for maintaining the payments API and fixing bugs."
   - *After:* "Cut payments API p95 latency from 820ms to 190ms by replacing N+1 ORM queries with batched PostgreSQL reads and adding Redis caching; checkout errors fell 37%."
   - *Why it works:* it turns a duty into a measured technical outcome with the stack named, so it's keyword-rich *and* evidential.

2. **Data analytics**
   - *Before:* "Created dashboards for the marketing team using Tableau."
   - *After:* "Built a Tableau attribution dashboard used weekly by 12 marketers; it showed paid social's CAC was 2.3× search, prompting a £180k budget shift in Q3."
   - *Why it works:* it shows the business decision the analysis drove, not just the artefact.

3. **Marketing (no hard numbers available)**
   - *Before:* "Managed the company's social media channels."
   - *After:* "Ran LinkedIn and Instagram for a 15-person B2B firm single-handed; introduced a weekly customer-story series that became the sales team's most-shared content."
   - *Why it works:* scope (single-handed, firm size) and a proxy outcome (adoption by sales) replace missing metrics honestly.

4. **Sales (SaaS)**
   - *Before:* "Exceeded sales targets and built strong client relationships."
   - *After:* "Closed £1.4m new ARR in FY24 (118% of quota, #2 of 14 AEs) selling to mid-market logistics firms; average deal £46k, 74-day cycle."
   - *Why it works:* sales readers look for attainment, rank, segment and deal shape, and it gives all four.

5. **Nursing (NHS)**
   - *Before:* "Responsible for patient care on a busy ward."
   - *After:* "Band 5 staff nurse on a 28-bed acute medical ward, coordinating care for 8 patients per shift; led the ward's falls-prevention audit, which cut recorded falls from 9 to 4 per quarter."
   - *Why it works:* band, ward type and patient ratio give scope, and the audit shows initiative with a clinical outcome.

6. **Teaching → L&D (career change)**
   - *Before:* "Taught Year 9 and 10 science classes and planned lessons."
   - *After:* "Designed and delivered a 12-week blended curriculum for 150 learners across 5 mixed-ability groups; redesigned assessments so 82% met target grades (department average 71%)."
   - *Why it works:* it translates teaching into learning-design vocabulary, with a relative outcome against a baseline.

7. **Finance / accounting**
   - *Before:* "Prepared month-end reports and reconciliations."
   - *After:* "Shortened month-end close from 9 to 5 working days by automating 14 bank reconciliations in Excel Power Query; zero audit adjustments in FY23."
   - *Why it works:* it gives before→after on the metric finance leaders care about, plus an audit-quality proof point.

8. **Project / programme management**
   - *Before:* "Managed multiple projects across the business."
   - *After:* "Delivered a £2.1m ERP migration across 3 UK sites on time and 4% under budget, coordinating 6 vendor and internal workstreams and 40 users through cutover."
   - *Why it works:* budget, scale, constraints met and stakeholder complexity in one line.

9. **Customer service / retail**
   - *Before:* "Dealt with customer complaints and queries."
   - *After:* "Handled ~60 calls a day in a 20-seat contact centre; ranked top 3 for first-contact resolution for 5 consecutive months and was chosen to train new starters."
   - *Why it works:* volume, rank and recognition quantify a role that "has no numbers".

10. **Trades (electrician)**
    - *Before:* "Carried out electrical work on various sites."
    - *After:* "Completed first and second fix on 30+ new-build homes per year for a regional housebuilder; 100% pass rate on NICEIC inspections; 18th Edition and ECS Gold Card."
    - *Why it works:* throughput, compliance record and tickets are exactly what site managers screen for.

11. **HR / people**
    - *Before:* "Assisted with recruitment and onboarding processes."
    - *After:* "Rebuilt the onboarding checklist and first-week schedule for 70 annual hires; new-starter 90-day attrition fell from 14% to 6%."
    - *Why it works:* it names the specific contribution and links it to a retention outcome.

12. **UX / product design**
    - *Before:* "Worked on redesigning the mobile app."
    - *After:* "Redesigned the app's sign-up flow from 7 screens to 3 after 12 usability sessions; completion rose from 41% to 63% (Figma prototypes, A/B tested with product)."
    - *Why it works:* it shows research → design → measured result, the full design-process story in one line.

13. **Graduate / student**
    - *Before:* "Member of the university hiking society."
    - *After:* "Treasurer, University Hiking Society (300 members): managed £18k annual budget, negotiated 15% coach-hire discount, and introduced online payments that ended cash handling."
    - *Why it works:* it turns a hobby into evidence of responsibility, negotiation and process improvement.

14. **Civil Service / policy**
    - *Before:* "Supported policy development on housing issues."
    - *After:* "Drafted 3 ministerial submissions on social-housing allocation and analysed 1,200 consultation responses, synthesising findings that shaped the final guidance published in 2024."
    - *Why it works:* concrete outputs, scale of evidence handled, and policy influence. It pairs well with the "Delivering at Pace" and "Seeing the Big Picture" behaviours.

15. **Executive**
    - *Before:* "Led the business through a period of significant change."
    - *After:* "As MD (P&L £64m, 420 staff, UK & Ireland), returned the business to profit in 18 months: exited 2 loss-making product lines, consolidated 5 depots to 3, and lifted EBITDA margin from −2% to 7%."
    - *Why it works:* scope line plus turnaround outcomes plus the specific levers. A board reads exactly this.

## 8. Critique of the predecessor's `formatting_rules.md`

**What it gets right.** Separating fixed layout code from variable content stops formatting drift between tailored versions. The output is a single-column, text-based DOCX with real bullet styles, Calibri 10.5pt, tab-aligned dates and rules, which parses cleanly. It analyses the JD first, uses exact terminology ("MySQL" not "SQL"), reorders skills and projects by relevance, and has a pre-generation checklist.

**What it gets wrong or misses**
1. **No honesty boundary; it invites fabrication.** The "Power Words" table says to *add* "99.9% uptime", "thousands of requests", "sub-second" and "reduced latency by X%" whenever the JD mentions reliability, scale or performance. That is inventing metrics to match the JD, and it is the most dangerous instruction in the file. Replace it with §4.1 and a `[CONFIRM]` workflow.
2. **US- and student-only by construction.** Letter paper, "City, State" and a fixed **Education-first** order suit a US student, not an experienced or UK candidate (A4, Experience first).
3. **No headline or profile**, wasting the most-read space.
4. **0.5" margins** are cramped and print-risky (Ladders found white space mattered).
5. **"Bold for ATS" is a myth.** Bold has no parser effect, and bolding every technology and metric destroys the hierarchy it is meant to create.
6. **"~20 spaces each side of [GitHub]"** is a fragile hack that renders inconsistently and injects space runs into extracted text. Use a tab stop or separator. The link text "[GitHub]" also hides the URL on paper and from parsers that drop hyperlinks; print the short URL.
7. **Tailoring as word substitution.** "Agile/Scrum → add 'daily standups'" produces filler. Tailoring means selecting and reordering evidence.
8. **No content rules**: nothing on achievement vs duty, quantification, bullet length, tense, clichés, AI tells, length or region. It is a typesetting spec, not a CV standard.
9. **No output checks**: no text-extraction test, PDF export, file naming, wrong-company check or page count.
10. **Prompt hygiene.** About 360 lines of mostly code is heavy context for an agent. Keep code in the script and rules in prose.

**Verdict:** keep the template mechanics, with 1.8–2cm margins, region-aware paper size, seniority-aware section order, a profile and printed URLs. **Delete the power-words table.** Add this document's honesty rules, rubric and region modifiers.
