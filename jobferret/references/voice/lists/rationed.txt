# rationed.txt
# What this is: words, phrases and punctuation that are fine once and a tell when repeated.
# Each entry is allowed AT MOST ONCE per document. A second hit is a failure of voice_lint
# check 3 (rationed count). Three or more categories over the limit means "rewrite from the
# facts, do not patch" (04 §7 step 3).
# Match semantics: one entry per line; lowercase; matched case-insensitively with word
# boundaries. An entry with no letters or digits ("!", ";" and the em dash) is matched as a
# plain substring. No entry depends on leading or trailing whitespace, so a loader may strip
# every line.
# Dash rule: the em dash is an entry below. A SPACED en dash ("word – word") and a spaced hyphen
# used as a dash ("word - word") count toward the same allowance, but they cannot be listed
# as literals without whitespace, so the linter matches them with the regex \s[–-]\s.
# Do not count a dash inside a date or number range ("2022–2024", "Mar 2022 – Present"), and
# do not run the dash check on --format cv-bullets.
#
# Optional grouping: lines that start with "# group:" name entries that share ONE allowance.
# Treat them as a combined count (for example "excited" plus "keen" plus "eager" is at most one
# in total). The per-entry rule still applies on top.
# group: feelings = excited | keen | eager
# group: hedged-belief = i believe | i feel
# group: intensifiers = really | very | truly | incredibly | extremely
# group: dashes = em dash | spaced en dash | spaced hyphen (one allowance in total)
#
# Per-format overrides (04 §4): "!" is zero, not one, in cover-letter and form-answer.
# ";" is rationed only in documents under 150 words.
# Source: 04 §4 "Rationed", 04 §3 (structural and tonal rows), 04 §7 step 3.
#
# Not literal strings, so the linter handles them separately (structural):
# structural: tricolon        a list of exactly three items (adjectives, nouns or clauses),
#                             "A, B and C"; at most one per document
# structural: antithesis      "not X, but Y", "it's not X, it's Y", "X rather than Y"; the
#                             literals below catch some, a regex over "not ... but" catches more
# structural: colon-payoff    a colon that sets up a punchline ("The result: a 30% drop.");
#                             a colon after a short setup clause followed by a fragment
# structural: rhetorical-q    a question the writer answers or does not expect answered; zero in
#                             cover-letter and form-answer (any "?" there is a flag); in email,
#                             hiring-manager, follow-up and thank-you the one "?" that is the ask
#                             is allowed, a second is not
# structural: trailing-ing    a clause after a comma that starts with a present participle and
#                             adds unearned importance (", ensuring seamless delivery")
# structural: recap           a final paragraph that restates the document (see closers.txt)
# structural: mirror          every paragraph has the same shape and length, within 20%
#                             (04 §7 step 7)
# structural: real            "real" or "really" as an intensifier is handled in agent-tics.txt
