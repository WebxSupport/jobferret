# Metric interview: pulling numbers out of the user

Use this in `cv-audit` after the rubric report, and in `apply` when a must-have is "listed only". Duty-style bullets ("responsible for...") become achievement bullets only when the user supplies scale, outcome or comparison, and only the user has them. Asking is the main lever for impact, and it is the honest alternative to inventing a figure (03 §2.4, §4.1 MAY 9). Source: (03 §2.4, §3.1).

Contents: 1 Ground rules · 2 Message format · 3 Question bank by bullet type · 4 Sector prompts · 5 Read-back and ledger · 6 When the user has nothing

## 1. Ground rules

1. **Batch, by role.** Send up to five questions per message (`ux.md` §2), grouped under the role they concern; usually two or three batches cover the CV. Do not ask one question at a time: it takes a dozen round trips and users give up. Do not send more than five: they skim.
2. **Aim at the weakest bullets first.** Start with the current or most recent role, then the next. Skip roles older than about 10 years unless the target needs them. Pick bullets with no outcome (rubric 4.1) and bullets that evidence a JD must-have.
3. **Quote the bullet** each group of questions is about, so the user knows which line you mean.
4. **Never propose a number.** Do not ask "was it around 20%?" That leads the answer. You may give magnitude buckets ("tens, hundreds or thousands?") or ask for the lower and upper bound.
5. **Accept approximate answers.** "Roughly halved", "about 60 a day", "somewhere between 10 and 15" are fine. Record them with `~` and use the cautious end of any range.
6. **Offer "I don't know / leave as is" every time.** State it in the message. A wrong number is worse than none, and pushing for one produces inflated figures. If the user does not know, add nothing to the ledger.
7. **Ask for the concrete thing as well as the number.** A specific noun (the system, the customer type, the site) is often worth more than a figure.
8. **Read each confirmed answer back as the exact text that will go in the ledger** (section 5) before you use it.
9. **No interrogation.** Two rounds per role at most. After that, write with what you have and mark the remainder as left as is.
10. **Neutral tone.** You are not testing the user. "This helps me show what you actually did" is enough.

## 2. Message format

```
Your CV is strongest where it shows results. These lines currently only describe the job.
Answer what you can. "Roughly" is fine, and "don't know" is a valid answer: I'll leave that line as it is.

**Acme Ltd, Senior Data Analyst** ("Created dashboards for the marketing team")
1. How many people use the dashboards, and how often?
2. What decision, if any, did a dashboard change, and roughly what was at stake?
3. How long did the reporting take before, and after?

**Beta plc, Data Analyst** ("Responsible for monthly reporting")
4. How many reports, to whom, and on what schedule?
5. Did you speed it up or fix errors? By how much, roughly?
```

Number the questions across the whole message so the user can reply "1: 12, weekly. 2: skip. 3: from two days to half a day."

## 3. Question bank by bullet type

Pick the 3 to 5 questions that fit the bullet; do not run through a whole table. Rephrase in the user's own vocabulary. Use their words for things (patients, learners, accounts, "jobs").

### People and teams
- How many people did you manage, supervise, train, mentor or coordinate? Direct reports or dotted line?
- How many were in the team, department or site you worked in?
- Who did you train or onboard, and how many people over what period?
- Which senior people or outside parties (clients, suppliers, clinicians, regulators) did you deal with regularly?
- Did you lead a project team or a shift without being the line manager? How big?

### Money and budget
- What budget, revenue, spend, caseload value or stock value were you responsible for?
- What did you save, recover, win or avoid, even roughly? Over what period?
- What was the size of the typical sale, contract, order or invoice?
- Did you negotiate a price, discount or terms? From what to what?
- Were you ever under or over budget on a piece of work, and by how much?

### Time and speed
- How long did the task take before you changed it, and after?
- How fast did you turn things around (response time, close time, delivery time) compared with the target or the previous norm?
- Did you deliver early or on a fixed deadline? Against what plan?
- How much of your own or the team's time did the change free up each week?

### Volume and frequency
- How many of this did you handle per day, week, month or year? (calls, patients, cases, orders, invoices, deliveries, lessons, tickets)
- How many customers, accounts, sites or users did you look after?
- How often did you report, present or run this, and to whom?
- What was the biggest or busiest example (peak day, largest job)?

### Quality and error
- What was the error, complaint, defect, rework or return rate before and after?
- What was your pass, audit, inspection, accuracy or satisfaction score?
- Was there an audit, inspection or review you went through? What was the result?
- How many incidents, near misses or breaches were there, compared with before?

### Growth and revenue
- What grew while you were responsible for it (followers, sign-ups, sales, footfall, applications, bookings)? From what to what, over how long?
- How did your result compare with the target, quota or previous year?
- What did you start, launch or build that did not exist before? Who used it, and how many?
- What was the conversion, retention or repeat rate before and after, if you tracked one?

### Rank and recognition
- Where did you rank among your peers (top 3 of 22, top quartile)? For how long?
- Did you win an award, a bonus, a commendation or a nomination?
- Were you promoted, given extra responsibility early, or asked to cover a more senior role?
- Were you chosen to train others, represent the team or present something? By whom?
- Did a manager, client or patient put something good in writing? (Ask only for the fact, not the quote.)

### Adoption and reach
- Who else used what you made (teams, sites, departments, customers)? How many?
- Did your process, template or tool become standard, or get copied elsewhere?
- Is it still in use? Who owns it now?
- Did it change a decision, a policy or someone's way of working? What changed?

## 4. Sector-specific prompts

Use these in addition to the bank above when the sector matches (03 §6 sector markers).

**Clinical (nursing, allied health, medicine)**
- Setting and size: ward or clinic type, beds, patients per shift, acuity, nurse-to-patient ratio.
- Registration and band/grade, with the number and expiry where checkable (NMC, GMC, HCPC).
- Audits, QI projects or incidents you led: topic, rate before and after, any trust-level adoption.
- Mentoring: students or new starters supported, any competencies signed off.
- Special skills and certifications: BLS, ILS, ALS, IV, prescribing, with dates.

**Teaching and education**
- Age range, subject, class sizes, total learners across groups, share with SEN/EAL or pupil-premium need.
- Results: your classes against school or department average, value-added, target-grade attainment.
- Responsibilities beyond the classroom: head of year, subject lead, exam board role, clubs, trips.
- Curriculum or assessment you designed, and who else adopted it.
- Inspection or observation outcomes, if you want them on the CV.

**Trades and technical**
- Tickets and licences (CSCS, Gas Safe, 18th Edition, CPCS, ECS) with levels.
- Job types and scale: new-build or refurbishment, number of units or properties per year, typical project value.
- Compliance: inspection pass rate, snagging rate, accident-free days, safety record.
- Supervision: apprentices or labourers you led, subcontractors you coordinated.
- Clients and sites: the main contractors, housebuilders or sectors.

**Sales and account management**
- Quota or target per year, and attainment (% or £). Say which years.
- Rank among the team, and the team size.
- Segment, average deal size, sales cycle length, win rate, pipeline you built.
- New logo versus expansion revenue; retention and churn on your accounts.
- Awards and clubs (President's Club, top performer) with the year.

**Public sector and policy**
- Grade or band, and the scope of the role (team, programme, budget, population affected).
- Outputs: submissions, briefings, consultations, reports, policies, guidance. How many, for whom (minister, board, committee)?
- Evidence handled: responses analysed, data sets, stakeholders consulted.
- Outcome: what was published, decided, funded or changed, and when.
- Which behaviours or person-specification criteria this evidences, if the user knows them.

## 5. Read-back and ledger

When the user answers, do this for each usable answer before writing it into the CV:

1. Turn the answer into the exact sentence or phrase you propose to use. Keep their numbers and any `~`.
2. Read it back: `I'll record this as: "Handled ~60 calls a day in a 20-seat contact centre." Is that right?`
3. If yes, append a ledger fact (`provenance: "user"`, `parent` = the bullet it extends, `note.asked` = your question, `confirmed: true`) following `rewrite-rules.md` §5. If they correct it before it is recorded, change the wording and read it back again. If they correct a fact already in the ledger, run `ledger_add.py --supersede F0xx --text "<corrected>" [--confirmed]`, cite the new ID it prints, and read it back. Never edit the JSON.
4. If the user says "I don't know", "skip", or does not answer, record nothing and leave the bullet as it was (with duty phrases tightened).
5. If an answer seems implausible for the title (a graduate with a £50m budget), ask once for clarification. Do not argue; if it stands, record it as stated and mark the entry for the user to be ready to defend in interview.
6. Where you combine answers arithmetically, show the sum ("9 to 5 days is a 44% reduction. Use that?") and cite both facts.

Do not present drafted bullets as final until each number in them has been confirmed this way. A figure still awaiting confirmation stays a `[CONFIRM: ...]` marker.

## 6. When the user has nothing

Some roles genuinely have few numbers. That is not a failure of the interview.

- Switch to scale, frequency and rank questions: they almost always exist.
- Ask for the concrete specific: the system, the customer type, the process changed, the person trained.
- Ask for a before and after in plain words, no numbers: "What was different after you did this?"
- If still nothing, tighten the duty bullet to a short, specific, true statement and move on. Never fill the gap with a number.
- Tell the user what they could track in their current role to have numbers next time (a one-line tip, once).
