# Bullet transformations: before to after

Twenty worked examples. Use them to calibrate what a good bullet looks like for a sector, not as text to copy. Source: (03 §5); examples 16 to 20 were added for sectors 03 does not cover.

**Every number below is illustrative.** Never carry a figure from this file into a user's CV. Real figures come from the user's CV or from their answers (`metric-interview.md`), are recorded in the ledger, and are cited (`rewrite-rules.md` §5). If the user has no number, use the techniques in the last section instead.

What the examples share: a concrete noun (a system, a customer type, a place), an action the person took, and a result or scale a reader can weigh. They differ in shape on purpose. Do not make every bullet "Verb + X, resulting in Y%" (03 §4.3).

## From 03 §5

**1. Software engineering (backend)**
- Before: Responsible for maintaining the payments API and fixing bugs.
- After: Cut payments API p95 latency from 820ms to 190ms by replacing N+1 ORM queries with batched PostgreSQL reads and adding Redis caching; checkout errors fell 37%.
- Why it works: a duty becomes a measured technical outcome with the stack named, so it is keyword-rich and evidential.

**2. Data analytics**
- Before: Created dashboards for the marketing team using Tableau.
- After: Built a Tableau attribution dashboard used weekly by 12 marketers; it showed paid social's CAC was 2.3× search, prompting a £180k budget shift in Q3.
- Why it works: it shows the business decision the analysis drove.

**3. Marketing (no hard numbers available)**
- Before: Managed the company's social media channels.
- After: Ran LinkedIn and Instagram for a 15-person B2B firm single-handed; introduced a weekly customer-story series that became the sales team's most-shared content.
- Why it works: scope (single-handed, firm size) and a proxy outcome (adoption by sales) replace missing metrics honestly.

**4. Sales (SaaS)**
- Before: Exceeded sales targets and built strong client relationships.
- After: Closed £1.4m new ARR in FY24 (118% of quota, #2 of 14 AEs) selling to mid-market logistics firms; average deal £46k, 74-day cycle.
- Why it works: sales readers look for attainment, rank, segment and deal shape, and it gives all four.

**5. Nursing (NHS)**
- Before: Responsible for patient care on a busy ward.
- After: Band 5 staff nurse on a 28-bed acute medical ward, coordinating care for 8 patients per shift; led the ward's falls-prevention audit, which cut recorded falls from 9 to 4 per quarter.
- Why it works: band, ward type and patient ratio give scope, and the audit shows initiative with a clinical outcome.

**6. Teaching to L&D (career change)**
- Before: Taught Year 9 and 10 science classes and planned lessons.
- After: Designed and delivered a 12-week blended curriculum for 150 learners across 5 mixed-ability groups; redesigned assessments so 82% met target grades (department average 71%).
- Why it works: it translates teaching into learning-design vocabulary and gives a relative outcome against a baseline.

**7. Finance / accounting**
- Before: Prepared month-end reports and reconciliations.
- After: Shortened month-end close from 9 to 5 working days by automating 14 bank reconciliations in Excel Power Query; zero audit adjustments in FY23.
- Why it works: before→after on the metric finance leaders care about, plus an audit-quality proof point.

**8. Project / programme management**
- Before: Managed multiple projects across the business.
- After: Delivered a £2.1m ERP migration across 3 UK sites on time and 4% under budget, coordinating 6 vendor and internal workstreams and 40 users through cutover.
- Why it works: budget, scale, constraints met and stakeholder complexity in one line.

**9. Customer service / retail**
- Before: Dealt with customer complaints and queries.
- After: Handled ~60 calls a day in a 20-seat contact centre; ranked top 3 for first-contact resolution for 5 consecutive months and was chosen to train new starters.
- Why it works: volume, rank and recognition quantify a role that "has no numbers". Note the `~`: the user's figure was approximate.

**10. Trades (electrician)**
- Before: Carried out electrical work on various sites.
- After: Completed first and second fix on 30+ new-build homes per year for a regional housebuilder; 100% pass rate on NICEIC inspections; 18th Edition and ECS Gold Card.
- Why it works: throughput, compliance record and tickets are exactly what site managers screen for.

**11. HR / people**
- Before: Assisted with recruitment and onboarding processes.
- After: Rebuilt the onboarding checklist and first-week schedule for 70 annual hires; new-starter 90-day attrition fell from 14% to 6%.
- Why it works: it names the specific contribution and links it to a retention outcome.

**12. UX / product design**
- Before: Worked on redesigning the mobile app.
- After: Redesigned the app's sign-up flow from 7 screens to 3 after 12 usability sessions; completion rose from 41% to 63% (Figma prototypes, A/B tested with product).
- Why it works: research, design and measured result: the full design-process story in one line.

**13. Graduate / student**
- Before: Member of the university hiking society.
- After: Treasurer, University Hiking Society (300 members): managed £18k annual budget, negotiated 15% coach-hire discount, and introduced online payments that ended cash handling.
- Why it works: a hobby becomes evidence of responsibility, negotiation and process improvement.

**14. Civil Service / policy**
- Before: Supported policy development on housing issues.
- After: Drafted 3 ministerial submissions on social-housing allocation and analysed 1,200 consultation responses, synthesising findings that shaped the final guidance published in 2024.
- Why it works: concrete outputs, scale of evidence handled and policy influence. It pairs well with the "Delivering at Pace" and "Seeing the Big Picture" behaviours.

**15. Executive**
- Before: Led the business through a period of significant change.
- After: As MD (P&L £64m, 420 staff, UK and Ireland), returned the business to profit in 18 months: exited 2 loss-making product lines, consolidated 5 depots to 3, and lifted EBITDA margin from −2% to 7%.
- Why it works: scope line, turnaround outcomes and the specific levers. A board reads exactly this.

## Additional sectors (written for this skill; not from 03)

These follow the same principles as 03 §2.4 but the specific examples are ours. Treat as illustrative.

**16. Legal**
- Before: Assisted partners with commercial contracts.
- After: Drafted and negotiated 40+ supply and distribution agreements a year for a 12-partner commercial team (3 years PQE); built a clause bank that cut standard-form turnaround from 5 days to 2.
- Why it works: volume, team context and PQE are what a legal recruiter screens for, and the clause bank shows a process contribution beyond the matter.

**17. Hospitality**
- Before: Supervised the restaurant floor during service.
- After: Ran the floor of a 90-cover restaurant, supervising 8 staff a shift; introduced a pre-service allergen briefing that took allergen complaints from 6 a month to 1 and lifted the online rating from 4.1 to 4.5.
- Why it works: covers, headcount and a quality outcome that matters in the trade; the briefing is a specific action, not "improved standards".

**18. Logistics / warehouse**
- Before: Responsible for warehouse operations and picking.
- After: Managed a 22-person day shift picking ~1,800 lines a day; re-slotted the 200 fastest-moving SKUs nearer dispatch, lifting pick rate from 95 to 120 lines per operative-hour and holding mis-picks under 0.3%.
- Why it works: throughput, team size and the exact lever pulled, with the productivity and accuracy metrics an ops manager reads first.

**19. Academia**
- Before: Published several papers and taught undergraduates.
- After: First author on 2 of 4 peer-reviewed papers; won a £12k departmental seed grant as PI; taught 3 seminar groups (60 students) a term, mean student evaluation 4.6/5.
- Why it works: authorship position, funding as PI and teaching load are the credibility markers of an academic CV; in a full CV, list the publications in their own section.

**20. Charity / third sector**
- Before: Helped with fundraising events.
- After: Organised 6 community fundraising events a year with a team of 5 volunteers, raising £38k (up from £22k); introduced Gift Aid sign-up at the door, adding £4.1k.
- Why it works: funds raised against a baseline, volunteer management, and a specific process change with its own return.

## Quantifying when there are no numbers

From (03 §2.4). Ask targeted questions (`metric-interview.md`), then use whichever kind of evidence is true:

| Kind | Examples |
|---|---|
| Scale or scope | People, users, budget, sites, accounts, patients per shift, countries |
| Frequency or volume | "~60 calls a day", "weekly reporting to the exec team" |
| Before to after | Time, cost, error rate, backlog, complaints. Honest approximations are fine ("roughly halved") |
| Rank or recognition | "Top 3 of 22 advisers", a promotion, chosen to train others |
| Proxy outcome or delivery | "Process adopted by 4 other teams", "delivered in 6 weeks against a 10-week plan" |

Rules:
- A **concrete specific** beats an invented number: "Rewrote the onboarding guide; new starters now shadow for 1 week instead of 3."
- Never round up. `~` marks an approximation the user confirmed, and the ledger text carries the `~` too.
- If the user does not know and nothing else is available, keep the bullet as it is and cut the vague words. A short true bullet beats a padded one.
- Derived arithmetic (a 44% reduction from 9 to 5 days) is allowed if you cite both source facts and show the sum in the change log.
- Sales is the one sector where no numbers is a failure: a sales CV without attainment fails (03 §2.6). Press for them.
